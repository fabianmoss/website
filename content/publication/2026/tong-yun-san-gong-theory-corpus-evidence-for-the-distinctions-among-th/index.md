abstract: >-
  This study investigates whether three commonly cited heptatonic modes ( diao ) in Chinese music theory, namely, zheng sheng , qing shang , and xia zhi , are empirically distinguishable in a large collection of folk melodies, taking tong yun san gong as a motivating, though contested, theoretical framework. We first provide manual annotations of diao type for melodies guided by structural distinction cues. We then evaluate a conservative label-blind baseline, an unsupervised Bayesian mode-inference model that represents each melody as a duration-weighted pitch-class distribution and learns a small set of latent mode profiles. Results show that, on the original corpus, the inferred mode profiles only partially align with the manual annotations, indicating substantial overlap among the three types when they are modeled with global pitch-class histograms alone. To clarify whether this overlap reflects corpus imbalance or within-category heterogeneity, we conduct two class-balanced resampling analyses. Balancing category sizes alone does not resolve the overlap, whereas smoothing synthetic profiles around category means markedly increases separability, indicating that within-category variability is the more substantial limitation in the original corpus. Overall, the findings suggest that the three heptatonic diao exhibit characteristic distributional patterns but that their recoverability from global pitch-class statistics in a naturally imbalanced folk song corpus is limited. The study provides an annotated benchmark and a theory-agnostic baseline evaluation of Chinese heptatonic diao distinction, and it motivates future work using richer sequential and structural features and broader, more representative corpora to characterize the Chinese heptatonic system.
